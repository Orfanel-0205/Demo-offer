# 7 · Cold-call script and objection responses

A friendly, low-pressure script for calling independent US businesses (restaurants, auto repair shops and other local services) for **Bright Site Tech**. The goal of the first call is **not to sell**. It's to get permission to send a sample.

---

## Before you dial

### Pick the right time

| Business | Best local time to call | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restaurants & cafés | **2:00–4:30 PM**, Tue–Thu | Lunch 11:30–1:30, dinner 5–8 |
| Auto repair & mechanics | **1:00–3:30 PM**, Tue–Thu | Drop-offs 7:30–10 AM, pickups after 4 PM, Monday mornings |
| Salons, barbers, home services | Early afternoon, mid-week | Their busiest booking times (check Google "Popular times") |

**2:00–4:30 PM local time is this time in the Philippines (PHT, next day).** For auto shops, start one hour earlier.

| Business time zone | US daylight time (mid-Mar → early Nov) | US standard time (early Nov → mid-Mar) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eastern (NY, FL, GA…) | 2:00–4:30 AM | 3:00–5:30 AM |
| Central (TX, IL…) | 3:00–5:30 AM | 4:00–6:30 AM |
| Mountain (CO, UT, ID…) | 4:00–6:30 AM | 5:00–7:30 AM |
| Arizona (no daylight saving) | 5:00–7:30 AM | 5:00–7:30 AM |
| Pacific (CA, WA, OR…) | 5:00–7:30 AM | 6:00–8:30 AM |

👉 **Pacific and Mountain** businesses fit a Philippine schedule best: early morning, not the middle of the night.

### Find one genuine observation

Spend 30 seconds on Google Maps, Yelp, Facebook or Instagram. Examples:

**Restaurants**
- "Your Google listing links to your Facebook page rather than a website."
- "The menu on Yelp is a photo of the paper menu, and it's hard to read on a phone."
- "I couldn't find your hours anywhere except an Instagram post from last year."

**Auto shops**
- "You've got a lot of good reviews on Google, but there's no website button on your listing."
- "I couldn't find which services you offer, or whether you work on [diesel / European cars / trucks]."
- "The only way to book seems to be calling, and your Facebook page has different hours from Google."

Only say something you actually saw. Never invent a problem, and never criticize their business.

**Have ready:** your website link and the gallery link for their type of business (e.g. `…/designs/?type=barber`, list in [guide 12](12-design-gallery.md)), a lead sheet, and your email address.

### Stay compliant and respectful

- Dial **manually**, one call at a time. No robocalls, no prerecorded messages, no autodialers, and no texting anyone who hasn't asked you to.
- If anyone says "don't call again," apologize, end the call, add them to your **do-not-contact list**, and never call again.
- Be honest about who you are, your business name, and where you're based if asked.

> Not legal advice: US telemarketing and spam rules (such as the TCPA and CAN-SPAM) and some state laws apply to sales outreach. Read the FTC's and FCC's plain-language guidance for businesses before you start.

## The script

### 1. Opener: ask permission, don't launch into a pitch

> **You:** "Hi! Is this the owner or the manager?"
>
> *(If no)* "No problem. When's a good time to catch them? … And what's their name?" → Thank them, note it, call back then.
>
> *(If yes)* "Hi [Name], my name is [Your name] with Bright Site Tech. I build simple websites for local businesses like restaurants and auto shops. I know you're busy, so is now an okay time for one quick minute, or should I call back?"

If it's a bad time: *"Totally understand. When's usually quieter for you?"* Note it, thank them, hang up.

### 2. The genuine observation

> "Thanks! I was looking at [Business] on Google, and I noticed [your genuine observation]."

### 3. The question

> "Do you have a website of your own right now, or is that not something you've needed so far?"

Listen. Let them talk. Their answer tells you which path to take.

### 4. The offer (only if they don't have a site, or are open to it)

**Restaurant version**
> "Got it. What I do is build a simple website that works great on phones: your menu as real text, your hours, directions, a call button, and links to whatever you already use, like DoorDash or OpenTable."

**Auto shop version**
> "Got it. What I do is build a simple website that works great on phones: the services you offer with starting prices if you want them, your hours, directions, a big call button, and a way to book or ask for a quote that goes to you."

**Then, for both:**
> "My Starter website is **$149, one-time**, and the Business version with a more custom design and a quote form is **$249**. The domain name is separate and stays in your name.
>
> I made a sample site for a made-up [restaurant / auto shop] so you can see exactly what it looks like. **Would it be okay if I sent you the link?** No obligation. Just take a look when you have a minute."

### 5. Get the details and close

> "Great. What's the best email for that, or would a text be easier? … And should I address it to you?"
>
> "Perfect. I'll send it over today. If it's not for you, no problem at all. Thanks so much, [Name]!"

Afterward, **send the follow-up within a few hours** ([guide 8](08-follow-up-email.md)) and log the call.

### If they say no at any point

> "No problem at all. I appreciate you taking the call. Have a great day!"

Then stop. No second attempt on the same call.

## Objection responses

Keep each answer short, agree where they're right, then hand the decision back to them.

**"We already use Yelp."** *(or "We're on Google already")*
> "That makes sense, and those are great for reviews. A website just gives you one place you control, with your current [menu / services] and hours, that you can link from Yelp, Google and Facebook. It doesn't replace them. Would it still be okay to send the sample so you can decide?"

**"We use Uber Eats."** *(or DoorDash, Grubhub)*
> "Good, and you'd keep using it. The site would have an 'Order on Uber Eats' button that goes straight to your store page. It doesn't replace the app or change how orders come in."

**"We don't need a website."**
> "That's fair. Plenty of places do fine without one. I'll leave it there. If it would be useful later, can I send the link so you have it on file? If not, no worries at all."
*(If they say no → thank them and end the call.)*

**"We get all our work from word of mouth."** *(common with mechanics)*
> "That's the best kind of work to get. Some shops still like having a simple page they can point people to, with hours, services and directions, so customers can check those without calling. But if you're happy as you are, I completely understand."

**"I don't want to put prices online."**
> "No problem at all, prices are optional. We can list your services and say 'call for a quote', or show starting prices for just a few common jobs. It's your call."

**"How much does it cost?"**
> "The Starter website is $149 one-time, with one round of changes. The Business website is $249, with a more custom design, a quote or inquiry form and two rounds of changes. The domain name is separate, usually paid yearly by you, and it stays in your name. If you want me to handle updates like prices or hours afterwards, that's an optional $39 a month. The exact scope is agreed in writing before I start."

**"Send me an email."**
> "Happy to. What's the best address? … And who should I put it to?"
*(Send it the same day. One follow-up later is fine; repeated calls are not.)*

**"We already have a website."**
> "Oh, great, then you're all set. Out of curiosity, are you happy with it, or is there anything you'd change, like updating [the menu / your services] or how it looks on phones?"
*(If they're happy: "Good to hear! Thanks for your time." If they mention a problem, offer to send the sample for comparison.)*

**"We're too busy."**
> "Totally get it, I'll be quick. Can I just send you a link to look at whenever things are quieter? Or is there a better day to call?"

### A few more you'll hear

**"Where are you calling from?"**
> "I'm based in the Philippines and work remotely with businesses in the US. Everything is done online, and you approve every detail before anything goes live."

**"How do I know this is legit?"**
> "Fair question. Nothing is paid until we've agreed on the scope in writing, and you see a full preview of your site before the final payment. I'm also happy to do a quick video call so you can see who you're working with."

**"Can you build online ordering / booking / payments into it?"**
> "I don't build payment or booking systems myself. Dedicated services do that better. What I do is connect your site to the one you already use, or one you choose, so customers find it easily."

**"Can I see sites you've done for other businesses?"**
> "I'm just starting out, so these samples are my main examples. That's why the prices are introductory. I'm happy to adjust the design until it feels right for you, within the agreed revision rounds."
*(Never claim past clients you don't have.)*
