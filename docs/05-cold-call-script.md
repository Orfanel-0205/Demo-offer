# 5 · Cold-call script and objection responses

A friendly, low-pressure script for calling independent US restaurants. The goal of the first call is **not to sell**. It's to get permission to send the sample.

---

## Before you dial

**Pick the right time.** Call during the slow hours, roughly **2:00–4:30 PM restaurant time**, Tuesday to Thursday. Never call during lunch (11:30–1:30) or dinner (5–8). Check the restaurant's hours on Google first.

2:00–4:30 PM restaurant time is this time in the Philippines (PHT, next day):

| Restaurant time zone | US daylight time (mid-Mar → early Nov) | US standard time (early Nov → mid-Mar) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eastern (NY, FL, GA…) | 2:00–4:30 AM | 3:00–5:30 AM |
| Central (TX, IL…) | 3:00–5:30 AM | 4:00–6:30 AM |
| Mountain (CO, UT…) | 4:00–6:30 AM | 5:00–7:30 AM |
| Arizona (no daylight saving) | 5:00–7:30 AM | 5:00–7:30 AM |
| Pacific (CA, WA, OR…) | 5:00–7:30 AM | 6:00–8:30 AM |

👉 **Pacific and Mountain** restaurants fit a Philippine schedule best: early morning, not the middle of the night.

**Find one genuine observation** (30 seconds on Google Maps, Yelp, Instagram). For example:

- "Your Google listing links to your Facebook page rather than a website."
- "The menu on Yelp is a photo of the paper menu, and it's hard to read on a phone."
- "I couldn't find your hours anywhere except an Instagram post from last year."
- "You have great reviews mentioning the [dish], but no menu online."

Only say something you actually saw. Never invent a problem.

**Have ready:** your demo link, a notepad or lead sheet, and your email address.

**Stay compliant and respectful:**

- Dial **manually**, one call at a time. No robocalls, no prerecorded messages, no autodialers, and no texting anyone who hasn't asked you to.
- If anyone says "don't call again," apologize, end the call, and add them to a **do-not-contact list**. Never call them again.
- Be honest about who you are and where you are if asked.

> Not legal advice: US telemarketing and spam rules (such as the TCPA and CAN-SPAM) and some state laws apply to sales outreach. Read the FTC's and FCC's plain-language guidance for businesses before you start.

## The script

### 1. Opener: ask permission, don't launch into a pitch

> **You:** "Hi! Is this the owner or the manager?"
>
> *(If no)* "No problem. When's a good time to catch them? … And what's their name?" → Thank them, note it, call back then.
>
> *(If yes)* "Hi [Name], my name is [Your name]. I'm a web designer and I build simple websites for independent restaurants. I know you're busy, so is now an okay time for one quick minute, or should I call back?"

If they say it's a bad time: *"Totally understand. When's usually quieter for you?"* Note it, thank them, hang up.

### 2. The genuine observation

> "Thanks! I was looking at [Restaurant] on Google, and I noticed [your genuine observation]."

### 3. The question

> "Do you have a website of your own right now, or is that not something you've needed so far?"

Listen. Let them talk. Their answer tells you which path to take.

### 4. The offer (only if they don't have a site, or are open to it)

> "Got it. What I do is build a simple one-page site: your menu as real text, your hours, a map and directions, a call button, and links to whatever you already use, like DoorDash or OpenTable. It's built for phones first, since that's how most people look up a restaurant.
>
> I'm taking on my first couple of restaurant clients at an introductory price of **$149, one-time**. The domain name is separate and stays in your name.
>
> I made a sample site for a made-up restaurant so you can see exactly what it looks like. **Would it be okay if I sent you the link?** No obligation. Just take a look when you have a minute."

### 5. Get the details and close

> "Great. What's the best email for that? … And should I address it to you?"
>
> "Perfect. I'll send it over today with the link. If it's not for you, no problem at all. Thanks so much, [Name]. Have a good service tonight!"

Afterward, **send the email within a few hours** ([guide 6](06-follow-up-email.md)) and log the call in your lead sheet.

### If they say no at any point

> "No problem at all. I appreciate you taking the call. Have a great day!"

Then stop. No second attempt on the same call.

## Objection responses

Keep each answer short, agree where they're right, then hand the decision back to them.

**"We already use Yelp."**
> "That makes sense, and Yelp is great for reviews. A website just gives you one place you control, with your current menu and hours, that you can link from Yelp, Google and Instagram. It doesn't replace Yelp. Would it still be okay to send the sample so you can decide?"

**"We use Uber Eats."** *(or DoorDash, Grubhub)*
> "Good, and you'd keep using it. The site would have an 'Order on Uber Eats' button that goes straight to your store page there. It doesn't replace the app or change how orders come in. It's just a home base people can find from Google or Instagram."

**"We don't need a website."**
> "That's fair. Plenty of places do fine without one. I'll leave it there. If it would be useful later, can I send the link so you have it on file? If not, no worries at all."
*(If they say no → thank them and end the call.)*

**"How much does it cost?"**
> "For my first couple of restaurant clients it's $149 one-time for a one-page site, including one round of changes. My standard package with a more custom design is $249. The domain name is separate, usually paid yearly by you, and it stays in your name. If you want me to handle menu or hours updates afterwards, that's an optional $39 a month. The exact scope is agreed in writing before I start."

**"Send me an email."**
> "Happy to. What's the best address? … And who should I put it to?"
*(Send it the same day. If they used it just to end the call, that's okay; one follow-up email later is fine, not repeated calls.)*

**"We already have a website."**
> "Oh, great, then you're all set. Out of curiosity, are you happy with it, or is there anything about it you'd change, like updating the menu or how it looks on phones?"
*(If they're happy: "Good to hear! Thanks for your time." If they mention a problem, offer to send the sample for comparison.)*

**"We're too busy."**
> "Totally get it, I'll be quick. Can I just email you a link to look at whenever things are quieter? Or is there a better day to call?"

### A few more you'll hear

**"Where are you calling from?"**
> "I'm based in the Philippines and work remotely with restaurants in the US. Everything is done online, and you'd approve every detail before anything goes live."

**"How do I know this is legit?"**
> "Fair question. You don't pay anything until we've agreed on the scope in writing, and I can show you a full preview of your site before you approve it. I'm also happy to do a quick video call so you can see who you're working with."

**"Can you build online ordering into it?"**
> "I don't build ordering or payment systems. For that, restaurants are better off with a dedicated service. What I do is link the site to the ordering service you already use, or one you choose, so customers can find it easily."

**"Can I see sites you've done for other restaurants?"**
> "I'm just starting out, so this sample is my main example. That's why the first couple of clients get the introductory price. I'm happy to make changes until the design feels right for you, within the agreed revision round."
*(Never claim past clients you don't have.)*
