# 8 · Plan for your first paying client in 30 days

A realistic, low-cost plan for a solo freelancer working from the Philippines with a phone and a ₱3,000 budget. It improves your odds, but it doesn't guarantee a client. Cold outreach is a numbers-and-quality game.

---

## Budget: ₱3,000 (roughly US$50, depending on the exchange rate)

| Item | Cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GitHub, Render static hosting, Gmail, Google Sheets, Google Meet, Squoosh, PageSpeed Insights | ₱0 | Free tiers cover everything for the demo. |
| VoIP calling credit for US calls | ~₱600–900 | Optional. Compare per-minute rates **and** whether your caller ID shows properly before buying. Unknown international numbers are often ignored, so email and Instagram DMs are free alternatives. |
| Your own business domain (e.g. for a professional email) | ₱0 now | Optional. Wait until your first payment arrives. Check the name isn't someone else's trademark first. |
| Payment account (PayPal, Wise or Payoneer) | ₱0 to open | Check each service's current fees for receiving US payments. |
| **Reserve** | **₱2,000+** | A second month of call credit, payment fees, surprises. |

Client domains are paid by the client (see [guide 2](02-connect-a-client-domain.md)).

---

## Who to target

Independent restaurants that are **clearly active** but **have no real website**:

- Google Maps listing with **no website**, or a "website" button that just goes to Facebook, Instagram, Linktree or a delivery app.
- **Recent reviews** (last few weeks) and a decent rating, so the business is alive and busy.
- Menu only available as photos on Yelp/Google, or not at all.
- Family-owned places: cafés, diners, pizzerias, taquerias, bakeries, Filipino/Asian/Latin restaurants.
- Skip chains and franchises (they have corporate sites) and places that look closed or for sale.

**Time zones:** focus first on **Pacific and Mountain** states (California, Washington, Oregon, Nevada, Arizona, Colorado, Utah, Idaho, New Mexico). Their afternoon slow hours fall in your early morning (see the table in [guide 5](05-cold-call-script.md)).

**Search ideas** (Google Maps on your phone): `restaurants in Fresno CA`, `diner Spokane WA`, `pizza Boise ID`, `café Tucson AZ`, `taqueria Bakersfield CA`. Mid-size cities often have many independents without websites.

## Week 1 (Days 1–7): Set up and build your lead list

- **Day 1:** Deploy the demo on Render ([guide 1](01-preview-and-deploy-from-your-phone.md)). Test every button on your phone. Add your business email to `site/agency.config.js` → `contact.email`.
- **Day 2:** Create a free business-style Gmail (e.g. *firstname.restaurantlaunch@…*). Add an email signature. Save the [follow-up email](06-follow-up-email.md) as a Gmail template.
- **Day 2:** Create a Google Sheet lead tracker with these columns:
  `Restaurant · City · State · Time zone · Phone · Email/IG · Google Maps link · Website status · Genuine observation · Contacted (date) · Channel · Result · Next step · Do not contact`
- **Days 3–5:** Add **100 qualified leads** (about 30–40 a day, roughly 2 minutes each). For each one, write the **genuine observation** you'll mention.
- **Days 6–7:** Practice the [call script](05-cold-call-script.md) **out loud** at least 10 times. Record yourself and listen: slow down, smile, sound like a person, not a script. Practice the [demo walkthrough](04-demo-walkthrough-script.md) on your phone until it takes under 2 minutes.

## Week 2 (Days 8–14): Start outreach

- **20 contacts per day**, split between:
  - **10 phone calls** during the restaurant's slow hours.
  - **10 written messages** to owners who list an email or Instagram. Keep them short, personal and with your genuine observation, and ask permission to send the sample rather than pasting the link uninvited.
- When someone agrees, send the follow-up email **the same day**.
- Log every contact. Mark "do not contact" immediately when asked.
- Expect lots of voicemails and "not now." That's normal, not failure.

## Week 3 (Days 15–21): Follow up and improve

- Keep adding **20 new contacts per day**. Add new leads as needed.
- Send **one** polite follow-up 5–7 days after the sample link, then stop unless they reply.
- Offer interested owners a **10-minute video call** (Google Meet) to walk through the demo on screen.
- End of the week, review your sheet: which observations and channels got replies? Which objections came up most? Adjust your opener.

## Week 4 (Days 22–30): Close and deliver

When an owner says yes:

1. Send a short **written scope** by email: package and price, what's included and excluded, one revision round, timeline, **50% to start and 50% on approval**, domain paid by them.
2. Send the [onboarding checklist](07-client-onboarding-checklist.md) and collect the essentials.
3. Build the site within 3–5 days using [guide 3](03-customize-for-a-client.md), and send the private preview link.
4. Do the one revision round, then get **written approval** and the final payment.
5. Connect their domain ([guide 2](02-connect-a-client-domain.md)) and send handover notes.
6. Ask, without pressure, whether they'd share honest feedback you may quote **with their permission**.

## Track your numbers (planning assumptions, not predictions)

Response rates vary a lot by city, timing and how personal your outreach is. Use these **illustrative** numbers only as a starting point and replace them with your own:

| Stage | Illustrative assumption | Your actual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contacts made (calls + messages) | 200–300 over 3 weeks | |
| Real conversations with an owner/manager | ~10–20% of contacts | |
| Agreed to receive the sample | ~25–40% of conversations | |
| Asked for a call / price / next step | a few | |
| **Paying client** | **1** | |

If your numbers are much lower after ~150 contacts, change **one thing at a time** (city, type of restaurant, opener, channel) and keep going.

## If you don't have a client by Day 30

- Re-read your notes from conversations. Was the problem reaching owners, the offer, or trust?
- Try a warmer channel: Filipino-owned restaurants in the US (shared language and culture can build trust), contacts from friends or family abroad, or local US business Facebook groups that allow service posts. Follow each group's rules.
- Keep the offer honest and the pressure low. One happy first client who agrees to be a reference is worth more than a fast but unhappy one.

## Rules that protect you

- Never claim past clients, results, or reviews you don't have.
- Never promise more customers, orders or Google rankings.
- Never publish a site with a real restaurant's name, menu or photos before they agree in writing.
- Never collect card details or passwords. Use proper payment services and the registrar's access-sharing features.
- Respect "no" the first time, and keep your do-not-contact list.
